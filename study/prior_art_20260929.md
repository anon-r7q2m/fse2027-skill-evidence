# Dated prior-art disclosure

Added 2026-09-29 after source/literature checking. This note supplements the
original study records; it does not change their inputs, pre-execution predictions,
observations or adjudications. A frozen prediction is not proof of global novelty
or absence of pretraining exposure.

## AutoGen source restriction (a21)

The missing `TextMentionTermination.sources` serialization field was publicly
reported before our study, against the same pinned base
`027ecf0a379bcc1d09956d46d12d44a3ad9cee14`:

- [PR 7837](https://github.com/microsoft/autogen/pull/7837), 2026-06-15,
  a draft proposal preserving the field and adding a round-trip regression.
- [PR 7933](https://github.com/microsoft/autogen/pull/7933), 2026-07-08,
  a related proposal that acknowledges PR 7837.
- [PR 8088](https://github.com/microsoft/autogen/pull/8088), 2026-08-23,
  another proposed fix of the omission.

All three were open and unmerged when inspected on 2026-09-29. They are not
three independent production incidents, accepted releases, or runtime evidence
produced by our study. Our a21 result is a controlled local reproduction of an
already reported defect. The nine-cell `native_integration/` study is a constructed
full-team consequence and compatibility check of that known mechanism. It is not
a new discovery; its U metadata failure and false global prerequisite remain.

## smolagents callback invocation under optimization (s12)

[Issue 2456](https://github.com/huggingface/smolagents/issues/2456), opened
2026-07-02, already reports that final-answer callbacks placed in an assertion
can be omitted with Python optimization. The associated public proposals include
[PR 2459](https://github.com/huggingface/smolagents/pull/2459) and
[PR 2469](https://github.com/huggingface/smolagents/pull/2469).
Our fixed native inputs reproduce the local consequence and test an explicit
invocation; Python assertion semantics and this framework defect are not claimed
as new. No upstream acceptance or merged status is inferred from these links.

## Self-generated tests and existing validation research

[Chen et al., ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-long.881/),
*Revisit Self-Debugging with Self-Generated Tests for Code Generation*, already
studies tests used to trigger and accept repairs, misleading feedback, and
contract-based validation of inputs. Validation-path weaknesses and architectural
mismatch also predate this study. Our recorded Django contribution is the concrete
repository distinction between a lookup-specific obligation, a shared helper,
and the caller's normalization. It is not discovery of self-testing bias,
contracts, or the general need to inspect validation infrastructure.
